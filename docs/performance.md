# Performance

How the app stays light, and how to measure it. Back to the [README](../README.md).

## Frame scheduling

The camera is locked to 30 frames a second at 640 by 480 where it offers that (`CameraFormatChoice`): the format with the fewest pixels from 640 by 480 to 1280 by 720 that reaches 30, uncompressed first, with both the minimum and maximum frame duration set to 1/30 s so a dim room cannot lengthen the exposure and halve the rate. AVFoundation on macOS has no control for a USB webcam's own "auto exposure priority"; a camera whose firmware still drops frames in low light (the log line "locked at 30 fps" is there, the panel's camera rate still shows fewer) needs more light. Center Stage is turned off for Ensemble's capture, because its cropping moves the head in the frame and breaks the calibration. The panel names both rates, "camera 30 fps · dot 75 Hz": the Logitech C920 offers no format above 30 fps, and the dot is redrawn on every refresh of the screen it is on regardless. A 60 fps camera (an iPhone through Continuity Camera offers one) is still locked to 30: the face tracker, the frame planner and the gesture timings are measured at 30 readings a second (gotchas, 4), and moving them is its own change.

Analyses are scheduled per frame. Hand pose runs on every frame only while a hand is in view and for 1 second after, while dictating, while a send is pending, while calibrating, or while a gesture is held. Otherwise it probes about 8 times a second, and with another app in front (where gestures do nothing) a hand in view gets only that probe too, about 27% of the hand frames, unless one of those full-rate reasons holds; switching back to Claude restores every frame on the next camera frame. The face tracker gets a 320 by 240 frame on every frame, up to 30 a second; only one frame is in flight, so a slow machine skips frames rather than queueing them. Hand pose runs on its own queue, one frame at a time, and a frame that arrives while it is busy skips hand pose only. It used to run on the camera queue, which holds no frames back (late frames are discarded), so every frame that arrived during hand pose was lost to the face tracker as well: 721 to 886 head readings in 30 s from a 30 fps camera instead of about 900. After 3 seconds with no face, both drop to about 2 a second until a face returns, never while dictating. The face tracker also drops to 10 a second while nothing needs the nose closely: another app is in front (Ensemble acts only on Claude), no Claude session is on screen, or you have been on the keyboard or mouse for 3 seconds (`FrameRatePlanner.wantsSlowFace`). It returns to every frame on the next camera reading or app switch once that ends, and never slows while dictating, sending, calibrating, in the tour or with a gesture in progress; hand pose keeps its own rules throughout. The log says each change ("face tracking at 10 Hz (another app in front)", "face tracking at full rate"). A camera restart (wake, a replugged camera) keeps the face tracker page loaded.

A still head skips the face tracker (`StillFace`). Before a face frame goes out, the camera frame is reduced to a 32 by 24 grid of luma means (8 by 8 pixels each, `FrameAnalyzer.lumaGrid`) and compared with the frame last sent; the change is the largest cell difference. After 3 frames in a row below 4 luma levels the frame is neither prepared nor sent, and the last reading is handed on again about 10 times a second, so the nose, the presence checks and the idle slowdown stay current. The first frame at or above it goes out at once, so movement after stillness waits no extra frame. A real reading still goes out at least once a second, nothing is reused from a reading older than 1.5 s (a tracker that stopped answering still looks stopped), and dictating, a send, calibrating, the tour's practice or a gesture in progress sends every frame. The grid covers the whole frame, not only the face, so movement anywhere in view (someone walking behind you, a flickering light) keeps the full rate: it fails toward more work, never toward a late dot. The log counts it every 30 s ("face frames in 30 s: 120 to the tracker, 780 skipped while still"); with `detailedTrace` on, each face frame's change and decision is traced. Measured with `perfbench web sec=60` (raw-pixel transfer): the face pipeline (harness, web content and GPU processes together) is 33.4% of a core at `hz=30` and 2.2% at `hz=1.2`, about the rate a still head sends.

CPU: the face tracker running every frame instead of every second frame roughly doubles the work of the web content and GPU processes (one landmark pass per frame); hand pose, brightness and the idle slowdown are unchanged.

## The coordinator tick

The coordinator's tick (the keyboard-and-mouse pause, the face rate, deferred focus, the dictation checks, scroll flushing, the Return windows, refusal expiry, the chat-column fit) runs on events: each camera reading (at most once per 45 ms), a key while the key watch is on, a headphone press, an app switch, a session scan and the 1 s refresh. A 20 Hz timer runs only while something in it is time-based and pending (`Coordinator.tickPending`): an Ensemble dictation or its start or stop, a send, a gesture or hold, a scroll, a resize, calibrating, the tour's practice, a deferred focus, a Return window, a refusal to expire or a fit waiting for focus to settle, and for half a second after. Starting the timer applies the face rate at once, so a gesture, dictation or send gets every frame without waiting. While dictating the timer runs exactly as before, and the dictation safety's deadman and standing check run on their own timer regardless. Idle (no face), the main thread wakes about 15 times a second (brightness readings at 10, head and hand at 2 each, the refresh) instead of about 35, and the tick rides those wakeups; a brightness reading ticks too, so the pause is never read less than 10 times a second while the camera runs. A model of the tick's hardware-input polls (`ps -o time` over 60 s) puts the always-on timer at 50 ms of CPU a minute against 10 ms event-driven: the gain is wakeups, not CPU.

A head or hand reading does not refresh the overlay itself (the session name, edge warning, highlight, nose dot goal, audio restore and status line): it marks it for one refresh 1/60 s later, which reads the latest values, so readings that land within that 1/60 s (head and hand together arrive up to 60 a second, both from each camera frame) share one refresh. Every other cause (an interrupt, dictation ending, a setting, Claude reporting a change, the tour, a stale reading) refreshes at once and clears the mark. The reading still arms the tick timer at once, so a gesture or hold it started never waits.

## Face frames

The face tracker page gets raw pixels, not an image file. The camera queue scales the frame to 320 pixels wide with vImage and swaps it to RGBA (`FrameAnalyzer.facePixels`), leaves it in a one-frame slot (`FaceFrameSlot`) under a number, and asks the page for that number; the page fetches `ensemble-asset://local/frame/<number>` from the app's own scheme handler, which hands the bytes out of memory once and empties the slot, and wraps them as ImageData for MediaPipe. It replaced a JPEG (quality 0.6) sent as base64 text inside `evaluateJavaScript` and decoded in the page. A frame is 307,200 bytes instead of about 8,900 bytes of base64, but nothing is encoded or decoded: on the bench below, 12.5 to 12.7 CPU ms per frame instead of 14.4 to 14.6 (about 13% less across the app, web content and GPU processes), half-way latency 14.6 to 16.5 ms instead of 18.7, and the web content process holds level (about 110 MB, no growth over 60 s). The frame never touches disk. Measured and not taken: BGRA wrapped as a VideoFrame (13.8 ms, no better than RGBA), raw bytes as a `callAsyncJavaScript` argument (WebKit refuses binary arguments), the CPU delegate (14.6 ms), and cropping the frame to the last face box (12.6 ms, but the landmarker's head matrix assumes the whole camera view, so head angles and position move with the crop, and its tracking lost the face on 58% of frames).

The landmarker computes only what the nose direction needs: one face, no blendshapes (that part is also cut from the bundled model), and the transformation matrix, which carries the head angles and the head position the pointer's sitting correction uses (gotchas, 12). MediaPipe's face detector alone (6 keypoints, already inside the landmarker bundle) costs about 9.0 CPU ms per frame instead of 12.5, but it gives no head position and only a flat 2D estimate of the turn, so it stays unused.

## Claude's accessibility tree

Reading Claude's tree costs Claude's own main thread a round trip per node. Claude tells Ensemble when its windows and panes change (`ClaudeWatcher`: accessibility notifications for window moves, resizes and new windows, divider and dictation-checkbox values, and destroyed windows, panes and checkboxes; NSWorkspace for Claude launching, quitting and coming to the front, and for Space changes). The session check still runs every second while a face is in view (every 5 seconds otherwise), but it reads only each cached dictation checkbox, one read per session, so a dictation Claude runs on its own is seen exactly as soon as before. The pane frames are read, one read per pane, only when a notification or the window server's list (which costs Claude nothing) says something moved; a burst of notifications (a window dragged, a divider moved) is folded into one read 100 ms after the first. A full walk runs when a window opens, closes or leaves the screen, a cached element is destroyed or stops answering, a pane changes size inside a window that did not (a pane opened or closed, or a divider moved), and otherwise every 15 seconds, the net for a notification Chromium did not post. Before, every check read the checkbox and the pane frame (three reads per session), any window move or resize walked the tree on every check while it lasted, and the walk ran every 10 seconds. Without the notifications (the observer could not be created), and during Ensemble's own width steps, the check works the old way (`SessionScan.plan`). A walk stops after 2 seconds, so a hung Claude cannot hold the bridge queue.

Measured on 2026-10-15 with a read-only harness that makes the same reads as the old and the new check, one to four sessions open (the owner was using Claude), two interleaved 126 s runs each: the time Claude spent answering fell from 12.9 and 5.4 ms a second to 4.8 and 3.1 (about half), and the harness's own CPU from 0.14 and 0.06% to 0.06 and 0.04%. Claude's whole-process CPU in `top` (2 s samples) moved more with what the owner was doing than with the check (7.8 to 8.2% with no check at all), so it cannot show a difference this small. The saving is modest because the walk skips the transcripts and costs a few hundred reads; the larger win is during a window drag or resize, which used to walk the tree every second until it stopped.

## The nose dot

Each head reading goes through a One Euro filter (`NosePointer`, strong smoothing when still and light when moving; the Settings slider picks how strong), then the rest rule (`NoseDot`: a resting dot ignores the nose until it is 32 pt away for 3 readings in a row, then follows it exactly and rests again where it stops), then a critically damped spring (`DotSpring`) that moves the dot's window on every screen refresh, so it glides between the 30 readings a second at 50 to 120 Hz. Focus decisions use the filtered reading, not the drawn dot.

The filter is restarted only when the calibration it maps through actually changes (a new calibration, another camera, a screen added, removed or moved). The camera engine repeats the camera in use with every frame-rate reading, about once a second, and each repeat used to re-decide the desk setup and refit the pointer, which restarted the filter: the next reading went through unsmoothed and the dot hitched about once a second. A repeated report is now ignored, a refit with the same samples keeps the filter (`SetupChangeFilter`, `NosePointer.fit`), and at launch the pointer is fitted from the saved profile of the camera used last instead of waiting for the camera's first report.

The dot's position and the highlighted pane are not published to SwiftUI: they change on every reading, and publishing them re-rendered Settings and the menu 30 times a second. Claude's process id is looked up at most once a second and the connected screens' ids once per screen change, not on every reading.

With `detailedTrace` on, the trace records how long each face frame held the gate and how many frames it turned away, how long hand pose took on each frame, and each frame whose hand pose was skipped.

`perfbench pointer [noise=0.004]` replays synthetic head paths through that pipeline at a 60 Hz display and reports jitter while still, the time to cover 90% of a 300 pt step, how far the dot rests from the aim, and judder while panning; `sweep` adds a grid of filter settings.

## Benchmarks

`Tools/PerfBench` is a separate Swift package of camera-free micro-benchmarks on synthetic frames and head paths. It never touches the camera or the installed app.

| Command | What it measures |
|---|---|
| `swift build -c release && .build/release/perfbench native` | Frame preparation and Apple Vision. |
| `perfbench web [transfer=pull, b64 or bgra] [sec=15] [hz=30] [delegate=GPU or CPU] [model=landmarker or detector] [max-cpu-ms=N] [max-growth-mb=N]` | The face pipeline on synthetic frames: frames answered per second, bytes per frame, latency, CPU ms per frame and CPU per second of the harness (frame preparation, as the app's camera queue), the web content process and the GPU process, and memory at start, peak and end. `pull` loads the app's own `head.html`; `b64` is the old JPEG protocol, for before and after. The limits make it exit 1, for a local check before a change to the face pipeline. |
| `perfbench pointer [noise=0.004] [sweep]` | How the nose dot moves: jitter, step lag, rest error and judder, before and after. |

Run them from inside `Tools/PerfBench`. The web bench needs a logged-in Mac with a GPU and is too noisy for a CI threshold (the same run moved from 30 to 25 frames a second under other load), so it is a local check: run `perfbench web sec=15 max-cpu-ms=15 max-growth-mb=20` before and after a change to the face pipeline and put both lines in the pull request.
